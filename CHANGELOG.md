# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Older factory snapshots are thinned out, per world and session, counted back from the world's newest save (not the clock, so a world nobody plays keeps its saves): every snapshot of the last 2 days stays, up to 30 days the newest per clock hour, up to a year the newest per calendar day, after that the newest per ISO week (UTC buckets). The newest snapshot and the one of the last import always stay. Thinning runs with every stored snapshot (import, "Add older saves", automatic upload) and in the hourly housekeeping; open timelines refresh. 30 saves a day for a year leave about 800 to 1,100 snapshots. "Ältere Spielstände hinzufügen" says so in one line ("Ältere Stände werden ausgedünnt: …"). The save upload's answer has `note: 'thinned'` (and `snapshot: false`) when an old save was thinned right away.
- Automatic upload: an upload interval in the script generator ("Höchstens ein Upload alle", default 30 minutes, 0–1440, 0 = no limit) for all three scripts. A save that comes in sooner waits; when the interval is up, only the newest waiting save is uploaded and the ones in between are skipped (in "Alle neuen Spielstände" mode logged as skipped). Only accepted uploads count, not duplicates, refused or failed ones. The time of the last upload is kept in the state file (`last-upload <seconds>` lines), so a restart keeps the limit. `FICSMAP_UPLOAD_INTERVAL_MINUTES` overrides it.

### Changed

- A world holds up to 2,000 factory snapshots (was 500); the limit is a safety net now and counts after thinning.

## [0.2.1] - 2026-11-08

### Added

- Admins per world: the owner can make members admins in the world settings ("Zum Admin machen", with a short confirm that says what admins may do) and take it back ("Admin-Rechte entziehen"). Admins may do what the owner may (import saves and older saves, delete factory snapshots, create, see and revoke upload keys, manage invite links, rename the world, change other members' routes, remove plain members), but not delete the world, change roles or remove other admins or the owner. An admin can give up the rights or leave the world. The member list shows each role as a badge (Besitzer / Admin / Mitglied), the world list too; a role change updates the affected user's view live. API: `PUT /api/worlds/:id/members/:userId/role` (`{role: 'admin' | 'member'}`); the world and member DTOs carry `role`.

### Changed

- The world DTO's `isOwner` is replaced by `role` (`owner`, `admin` or `member`).
- Upload keys stop working when the user who created them loses admin rights, leaves or is removed from the world.
- `DATASET_VERSION` defaults to `latest`: the server serves the newest dataset folder named like a game version (e.g. `1.2.5.0` over `1.2.4.1`), so a new dataset can be copied next to the old one and is used after a restart; a folder name still pins one. Before, the server refused to start with more than one dataset folder unless the version was set.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-08

### Added

- A favicon (an orange map pin with a hex nut, SVG with ICO and Apple touch icon fallbacks), also on the invite page.
- Caves: a map layer (layer switch "Caves" on the map) with the floor plans of the caves and their entrances, and a "Cave" section in the item panel for items inside a cave, with the distance to the nearest entrance. The caves are sampled from the game's 3D geometry (rock meshes, cave tunnel splines and landscape), located by the game's cave sound and fog volumes; entrances are where cave floor meets walkable open ground, one per opening. An item counts as in a cave if it lies on cave floor or deep under the landscape inside a cave volume (148 in 1.2.5.0, among them every collectible more than 5 m underground in a cave volume); caves whose hall lies under the landscape without a ceiling of its own in the geometry are found too. Needs a dataset built with the extractor's `caves.json` and cave geometry (patch flags `--caves-out`, `--cave-geometry-out`); older datasets work as before without the layer.
- Height layer: a layer switch "Height map" (with its own contour-line icon) colours the map by the height of the highest surface (one blue scale, light = low, dark = high, fixed from -170 m to 470 m), with a legend. Zoomed out it is drawn from an 8 m overview, zoomed in from the 2 m tiles.
- Vehicle calculator (the "Vehicles" section): from the route length, the vehicle (Tractor, Truck, Explorer, Fluid Truck, Drone, Freight Car), the average speed and the stop time it computes what one vehicle moves per minute and how many vehicles a target throughput needs (rounded up, with the utilisation), and an estimate of the fuel they burn per minute and per round trip. Self-driving wheeled vehicles burn fuel per distance driven (5 MJ per metre; any solid fuel, Packaged Fuel by default; the extra use of the Tractor and Explorer on climbs is not included); the drone burns a fixed amount per round trip plus some per metre flown (in MJ as players measured it) and takes its own seven fuels, Packaged Fuel by default. Without an item the result is in stacks per minute, with an item in items and stacks per minute, with a fluid in m³ per minute. Inventory slots, fluid capacities, stack sizes, fuel use and energy values and the stop times of Truck Station, Fluid Truck Station, Freight Platform and Drone Port come from the game's Docs files and paks. The speed defaults to the game's limit: the autopilot's straight-path limit, or the lower speed at which the engine's tractive effort ends (Tractor 70.6 km/h, Explorer 100.4 km/h), and for the drone the travel speed of its fuel; for the train you enter it. Needs a dataset built with `transport.json` (Docs folder plus the extractor's new `--transport-out`); older datasets show a notice instead.
- Vehicle calculator: start and destination on the map. "Pick on the map" hides the calculator and a banner asks for the start, then the destination; a click (or tap) on the map sets the point, a click on a marker uses the marker's position (where markers are icons; zoomed out a click is just a point). Esc or Cancel leaves and brings the calculator back. A and B and the straight line between them stay on the map, also with the calculator closed, and can be dragged; "Clear points" removes them. The calculator shows the straight-line distance and the height difference (from the height map, if the dataset has one) and fills the route length with the straight line: exact for drones, a lower bound for wheeled vehicles on roads, so the length stays editable. Moving a point updates the length until you type one; a new pick always sets it, and "Use the straight line" takes it back. A new block shows what one vehicle does on the route: the one-way trip, the round trip with the stops, the stacks per minute (and items per minute with an item, m³ for fluids), the load per trip and the fuel per round trip; for the drone, whose speed depends on its fuel, a table compares all its fuels (speed, one-way trip, round trip, fuel per round trip, stacks per minute) with the selected one marked. The fleet answer gives the vehicles needed and their fuel per minute. On mobile the panels sheet drops to its smallest size while picking, so the map is free to tap. Real paths along roads, belts or rails are left for a later route planner.
- Extractor patch: `--icons-out` writes the game's icon of every item, building and vehicle descriptor the Docs list (747 in 1.2.5.0) as WebP at 64 and 256 px, with an index; the dataset build copies those the wiki uses (part `icons`, about 240 pictures, 4 MB).
- Extractor patch: `--transport-out` writes the vehicle values that are only in the paks (fluid stack multipliers, the drone's docking and take-off durations and its fuels with their travel speeds, the autopilot's straight-path speed limit and the speed where the tractive effort ends).
- Extractor patch: `--map-areas-out` writes the game's own map area raster with the area names in English and German (it turned out not to match the known biomes and is kept for reference).
- Coordinates under the mouse: bottom left on the map, X and Y in metres and Z, the height of the highest surface there (terrain, rocks, structures; no foliage). Z needs a dataset built with the extractor's height grid (patch flag `--heightmap-out`); it is loaded in 500 m tiles while you hover.
- Extractor patch: `--heightmap-out` writes height grids of the whole map (highest surface at 2 m, the landscape alone, foliage, and the game's own `HeightData_Test` texture).
- Extractor patch: `--cave-geometry-out` writes the 3D geometry around every cave as glTF binary files (one per cave), usable for 3D previews.
- Search in the middle of the top bar (`/` or Ctrl+K): collectibles by type name in English and German ("Somersloop", "blau"), by their label ("Dune Desert 3") or actor ID (`BP_WAT139`, also partial), biomes and caves by name (in both languages), and crash sites by what lies there, its threats or what the pod needs ("Modular Frame"). Case and accents do not matter; exact IDs come first, then prefixes, then other matches. While typing, the matches are highlighted on the map (a halo, drawn on top even if a filter hides them) and everything else fades. Choosing an item selects it and zooms to it, a biome scopes the map to it, a cave switches the cave layer on and fits the map to it, a crash site selects its pod. Arrow keys, Enter and Esc work as in any combobox; on mobile the search opens over the top bar from a button. Other features can add their own results (e.g. recipes later) through the new `searchProviders` extension point.
- Navigation inside a world: from 1280 px the sections are tabs under the top bar (Map, Planner, Vehicles, Machines; icon and word, the active one underlined in orange), on the map with its layer switches (Caves, Height map, Resource nodes) at the right end; below 1280 px and on phones one menu named after the section shown holds the sections and, on the map, the layers. The layer switches and Import show only on the map; page headings use the tab's word. Features add tools through the new `tools` extension point; a layer switch moves there with one line (`toggleTool()`).
- Resource nodes: a map layer (layer switch "Resource nodes" on the map) with all 459 resource nodes, 31 geysers and the 17 resource wells with their 118 extraction points. Each node shows the game's picture of its resource (geysers, which have none, and nodes whose picture has not loaded yet show a disk in the resource's colour with a short label such as Fe or Cu) in a ring in the colour of its purity: impure red, normal orange, pure green. A resource well's centre has a dashed neutral ring (it has no purity). Hovering or clicking a node shows resource (with its picture), purity (in its colour) and position, plus what each fitting extractor gets out of the node at 100 % clock speed, e.g. "Pure · 120/min (Mk.1) · 240 (Mk.2) · 480 (Mk.3)", "240 m³/min (Oil Extractor)" or for a well's extraction point "120 m³/min (Resource Well Extractor)" (rates from the dataset's `recipes.json`, purity factors ×0.5, ×1, ×2 from the Satisfactory wiki; geysers make power, no items, and show none; without `recipes.json` no rates are shown). A "Resource nodes" panel in the sidebar filters by resource (each with its picture; Alt-click: only this one) and purity (in its colour, with the miners' range at 100 %, e.g. "30–120/min" for impure, and all extractors' rates on hover), with counts; the layer and its filters are remembered on the device. A save import also reads the save's resource and purity per node where the save sets one and the world shows those instead of the default map; nodes changed that way get a light dot on their ring. A save without randomisation stores no purity at all, and the world keeps the default map. A world started with randomised nodes whose save holds only the settings and seed keeps the default map too; the panel and the import dialog then say so and name the settings as the game does (e.g. "Resource Node Randomization: Basic Resource Rich", in German "Zufällige Rohstoffvorkommen: „Reich: Grundressourcen“"). Resource names follow the game's own display names (German e.g. Caterium-Erz, Rohstoffvorkommen). Needs a dataset built with the extractor's `default-world.json` (`resource-nodes.json`); older datasets work as before without the layer. From zoom 2 a node looks like a collectible marker and has its size: the resource's picture (32 px) on a see-through plate in a 2 px ring in the purity colour.
- Resource nodes: after a save import the map shows which extractor stands on which node. Occupied nodes get a tag with the building (Mk.1, Mk.2, Mk.3, Oil, Well, Pres for the resource well pressurizer, Geo, Port for portable miners; 12 px, readable from zoom 4) and one diamond per Power Shard; the node's info box lists building, clock speed and Power Shards as the save has them (portable miners, geothermal generators and resource well extractors cannot be overclocked and show neither; a well's clock speed and Power Shards are on its pressurizer), and what the building gets out of the node at that clock speed, e.g. "Extracts 360/min" for a Miner Mk.2 at 150 % on a pure node (a resource well extractor at its pressurizer's clock speed; portable miners have no rate in the game data and show none). The "Resource nodes" panel gains an "All / Occupied / Free" filter with counts and a summary of the extractors per building and the Power Shards in them. The import sends these placements (node, building, clock speed, Power Shards) to the server, where they are stored per world and shown to every member; without an import nothing changes.
- Production planner (the "Planner" section): enter one or more items and how many per minute you need; the chain is resolved down to the extractors (miners, water and oil extractors, resource well extractors). The recipe can be switched per item, including alternates, by-product recipes and "supplied from outside", and so can the miner (Mk.1, Mk.2 or Mk.3; the extractor setting is the default). By-products count against what other steps need (Heavy Oil Residue from Plastic feeds Residual Fuel), so those steps make only the rest; what is left over is listed as surplus. Picking an unpacking recipe (Unpackage Fuel) supplies the packaged item from outside automatically instead of looping. Settings, folded with the values in effect: miner level (Mk.1/2/3), extractor clock speed and node purity (impure ×0.5, normal ×1, pure ×2, factors from the Satisfactory wiki); every step can have its own clock speed (1 % to 250 %, with the Power Shards it needs) and node purity, production buildings included; power follows the game's power exponent. The minimum clock speed and the +50 % per Power Shard come from the game data, the 3 shard slots from the wiki. A resource well runs at the clock speed of its pressurizer, which applies to all its extractors: the Power Shards go into the pressurizer, and its power (150 MW at 100 %, scaled with the clock speed) is noted per well, apart from the total. Values are for the default game mode (game mode settings such as recipe costs are not taken into account). Loops in the recipe chain (e.g. Recycled Rubber and Recycled Plastic) are solved and marked; loops that use up as much as they make are reported. Compact totals sit beside what you need, laid out in columns so a plan with many resources stays short: the power (a range for the Particle Accelerator, Converter and Quantum Encoder), the Power Shards of all buildings when a step uses some, the raw resources with their extractors ("40× Miner Mk.1") and, for resources mined on nodes, "Show on map" (the map with only that resource's nodes of the plan's purity), items supplied from outside, surplus, and the buildings by kind folded under their total. The steps are a material flow from top to bottom: the raw resources and their extractors at the top, the wanted items at the bottom (marked), every item once. Each step is a small card: the game's picture of its building with the product's picture pinned to its corner (an item supplied from outside shows the item in a dashed frame), the buildings as in "4× Constructor" ("4× Konstruktor" in German; the exact count as tooltip), the item and its rate (fluids in m³/min, by-products on a line of their own) and badges for the alternate recipe, a node purity that differs from the setting, the clock speed and Power Shards; each row is as high as its tallest card, so the Heavy Modular Frame chain fits about one screen. The rates on the arrows carry the item's picture, by-products are dotted and loops dashed, the arrows of the hovered or selected step stand out, and a legend appears only for by-products, loops and items supplied from outside. Selecting a step opens it like the game's machine window, beside the diagram on a wide page and below it on narrower ones: building and count with the exact count and the clock speed for whole buildings ("exact 52.5 · 53× at 99.06 %"), the recipe with its picker, the inputs, the production cycle and the outputs (each per minute for all buildings and per building), the clock speed as slider and field with the three Power Shard slots filling as it needs them, the power for the step and per building, and for extractors the node purity and the extraction rate; Escape closes it, the arrow keys move between the steps. A "Diagram | List" switch in the title bar of the steps shows them as a compact table instead (a row opens the same window; its input chips name their item, "Steel Ingot 300/min"; remembered); phones start with the list, the diagram there opens with whole nodes of the top row in view, and from 640 px it can be fitted to the width. "Start over" sits in the title bar of "What do you need?". "Start over" can be undone from its toast. An empty planner says what it will show, notes the default game mode and offers an example (Reinforced Iron Plate, 5/min). Screen readers hear the totals as one short sentence when they change. Names in English and German; the plan is remembered on the device. Needs `recipes.json` in the dataset, built from the game's `CommunityResources/Docs` (`npm run dataset -- ... --docs <dir>` or `npm run recipes`); older datasets work as before, the planner then says the data is missing.
- Resource nodes in the header search: one result per resource with its nodes by purity ("Iron Ore · 46 pure · 42 normal · 39 impure"), and one per resource and purity ("Iron Ore · Pure", also found by "iron pure" or "eisen rein"). Choosing one switches the resource node layer on with only that resource (and purity); the collectibles keep their colour, so drop pods next to the nodes stay visible.
- Machine and vehicle wiki (the "Machines" section): one entry per production building, extractor, generator, other powered machine and vehicle, grouped by category and searchable by English or German name. Each entry shows its stats (power use or production, clock speed range where the game lets you change it, Somersloop slots, extraction rate on a normal node, fuels per minute with water and waste, the Alien Power Matrix per minute with its boost, the geothermal average on a normal geyser, inventory or storage size, fuel burn, ...), the game's own description, the build cost, what unlocks it, the recipes that run in it and a link to the page on the Satisfactory wiki. All values come from the game's `CommunityResources/Docs` files; what they do not contain is left out. Needs a dataset built with `--docs` (`machines.json`, also `npm run machines`); without it the wiki says so. Machines and vehicles are also found by the header search (English or German name, class), and choosing one opens its wiki entry.
- Machine wiki: pictures and a tier filter. Every machine and vehicle shows the game's own picture: as a card in a grid of pictures grouped by category (each category with its own colour), large in the entry's header, and small in the list beside an open entry. Items carry their picture too, in build costs, recipes, fuels and extracted resources. A tier filter above the list selects one or more tiers (0 to 9, the HUB upgrades counting as tier 0), MAM research and the AWESOME Shop; "Up to tier" (or Shift-click on a tier) selects everything up to that tier in one step, e.g. tiers 0 to 4. It combines with the search, shows how many entries match and is remembered on the device. Each entry carries a badge with its tier (or MAM, Shop). The pictures come from the game's files only (dataset part `icons`, from the extractor's new `--icons-out`), at 64 and 256 px so they stay sharp on high-density screens, and load lazily; a dataset without them shows the wiki as before, without pictures.
- Factory snapshots: every save import also stores the save's factory per world (buildings with recipe, clock speed, Power Shards, Somersloops, productivity and what holds them back; foundations and other lightweight buildables; belts, pipes, hypertubes and roads; power lines and circuits; belt and pipe connections between machines with the items on the belts; stations with traffic and cargo; vehicles with routes, fuel and cargo; map markers, crates and the explored area), as the base of the factory timeline. No player names or positions. "Add older saves" in the import dialog and the world settings fills the timeline from several saves at once without touching checks or resource nodes; `npm run inspect-factory -- <save>` prints a save's snapshot and production lines.
- Fabrik page (the "Factory" section after the map): a timeline of the world's saves with compare; figures (machines, mean productivity, stopped, part load, power capacity) with changes in points; "Größter Engpass" with links to the diagram and the map; inefficient items with shortages first and surplus last, ranked by lost machine-equivalents, each with its machines, reasons (Mangel, Stau, Überschuss, kein Strom, kein Treibstoff, pausiert) and the cause along the chain (consumers, where a jam ends, a full belt, a connection to check, or missing demand); miners with node purity, actual of target, the belt at their output and hints (belt too slow, shared belt full, no belt, overclocking does nothing right now); machines by type with filters; stations and vehicles with cargo, items per minute, fill, intervals, empty legs and the consumers at unloading stations; changes between two saves in rows (machines, logistics, foundations).
- Production lines: the factory's machines grouped by their real belt and pipe connections, with parallel machines merged per stage, the estimated flow and the capacity of every connection (belt labels like "4× Mk.1 (je 60)", pipeline capacities from the game data) and each line's bottleneck in one sentence that names the missing input and what to do. On the Fabrik page as a list and a top-down diagram like the planner's ("Nur Maschinen" for large lines, by-products folded, fit to width, a group's machines with "Auf der Karte"); on the map pointing at a machine, belt or pipe lights up its whole line with the connections coloured by load and the flow direction, and a line card links to the diagram ("Diagramm | Karte" both ways, `?line=<id>`).
- Fabrikplan on the map: everything built from the saves as a 2D top-down sketch from the game's own building sizes (floor plan, buildings with pictures and the item they make, belts and pipes by tier, power lines, roads, stations, vehicles, markers, crates, explored area), coloured by productivity (problems stand out with patterns and glyphs, full machines recede) or by category; info windows for buildings, stations and vehicles; a timeline at the bottom to step through the saves with "Änderungen" (new, removed, better, worse; stepping through the areas of change). The map zooms to level 10 while the plan is on.
- Automatic upload: the world owner creates upload keys (stored hashed, shown once, revocable) and downloads a configured watcher script for Windows (PowerShell), Linux (bash) or Docker in the world settings ("Automatischer Upload"). The script uploads every new save to `POST /api/worlds/:id/saves`; the server parses it in a worker thread, imports saves newer than the last import, adds older ones to the timeline and skips ones it already has. Behind nginx, `client_max_body_size 64m` is needed.
- Dataset part `buildings` (`buildings.json`): every buildable and vehicle with English and German names, category, footprint from the game's clearance data (extractor flag `--buildings-out`) and picture; the `icons` part copies all 747 pictures; `recipes.json` gains stack sizes, belt speeds and pipeline flow limits from the Docs.
